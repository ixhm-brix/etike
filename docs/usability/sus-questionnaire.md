# System Usability Scale (SUS)

The SUS is a standard 10-question usability questionnaire (John Brooke, 1986), free to use. Give it to each participant right after the tasks, on paper, before discussing anything.

For each statement, the participant circles one number:
**1 = Strongly disagree, 5 = Strongly agree.** Ask them to answer every item; if unsure, circle 3.

| # | Statement | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|-----------|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I think that I would like to use this system frequently. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 2 | I found the system unnecessarily complex. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 3 | I thought the system was easy to use. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 4 | I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 5 | I found the various functions in this system were well integrated. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 6 | I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 7 | I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 8 | I found the system very cumbersome to use. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 9 | I felt very confident using the system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| 10 | I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system. | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ | ○ |

"System" means the ETIKE website. If you translate the statements into Kinyarwanda, keep the meaning and the order exactly the same, and use the same translation for every participant.

## Scoring

1. **Odd items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9):** contribution = answer − 1
2. **Even items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10):** contribution = 5 − answer
3. Add the ten contributions (0 to 40) and **multiply by 2.5**. The SUS score is 0 to 100.

Example: answers 4, 2, 5, 1, 4, 2, 4, 2, 4, 1 give contributions 3, 3, 4, 4, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 4 = 33, so the score is 33 × 2.5 = **82.5**.

The score is not a percentage. As a rough guide, 68 is about average; above 80 is good.
